# ⚽ English Premier League Football Analytics

This project explores match data from the English Premier League (EPL) between 2000 and 2025. Using Python and data visualisation tools, we uncover insights into team performance, goal trends, fouls, and more.

## 📊 Dataset
- **Source**: [Kaggle - EPL Match Data 2000–2025](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/marcohuiii/english-premier-league-epl-match-data-2000-2025)
- **File Used**: `epl_final.csv`
- The dataset includes match results, goals, cards, shots, and other key statistics.

## 🛠️ Tools & Technologies
- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Seaborn
- Jupyter Notebook

## 🏠 Home Advantage Spotlight: 2009/10 Season

The 2009/10 Premier League season stands out as the only year between 2000/01 and 2024/25 where:

- Home teams were more efficient than away teams in converting shots to goals
- Home teams scored **61% of all goals**, the highest share in the dataset

This anomaly may be explained by dominant home performances from top clubs like Chelsea, who scored 52 goals at home and won 17 of 19 home matches. 

Also, games played against weaker teams by the top teams that finished that season included unusually lop-sided scores like Wigan losing 8-0 to Chelsea and 9-1 to Tottenham Hotspurs.

### 🔴 Red Cards Had Minimal Impact
Contrary to expectations, matches with red cards did **not show a significant change** in shot volume. While red cards are often assumed to disrupt team performance, the data suggests that their overall impact on attacking output was negligible across seasons.

### ⚠️ Least Efficient Seasons for Goal Scoring
The poorest goal efficiencies—where teams needed the most shots to score—occurred in:
- **2008/09**: A season marked by tighter defences and lower conversion rates
- **2013 to 2016**: A multi-year stretch where attacking efficiency dipped
